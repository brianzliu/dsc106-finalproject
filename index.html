Projected Earth Close-Approaches (2026 - 2125)

Initial Prototype Writeup

1. What have you done so far?

Our team, The Pickle Farmers, has finalized our dataset selection, utilizing the NASA Near-Earth Asteroids Dataset to explore orbital mechanics and impact risks. We have established our GitHub repository, deployed our initial GitHub Pages site, and laid out the structural HTML and CSS foundation for our explorable explanation. During the proposal phase, we mapped out the five core visualizations that will anchor our narrative, ranging from a global top-down solar system rendering to a detailed timeline of projected Earth close-approaches. Currently, we are initializing the basic Three.js scene for the 3D space rendering and configuring the D3.js framework to handle our 2D data overlays and tooltips.

2. What will be the most challenging part of your project to design and why?

The most challenging aspect of our project will undoubtedly be seamlessly integrating the 3D Three.js environment with the 2D D3.js interactive scrollytelling elements. Rendering hundreds of orbital trajectories simultaneously poses a significant performance risk, so we will need to carefully optimize our data handling to prevent browser lag. Additionally, choreographing the camera transitions—moving fluidly from a macro view of the near-Earth asteroid population down to an isolated orbit—will require complex state management and spatial mathematics. We anticipate that keeping the user oriented during these dynamic zooms, while simultaneously updating the informative tooltips and annotations, will require heavy iteration on the user experience design.
