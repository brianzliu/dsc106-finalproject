Initial Prototype Writeup

1. What have you done so far?

Our team, The Pickle Farmers, has finalized our dataset selection, utilizing the NASA Near-Earth Asteroids Dataset to explore orbital mechanics and impact risks. We have established our GitHub repository, deployed our initial GitHub Pages site, and laid out the structural HTML and CSS foundation for our explorable explanation. We built a data pipeline that cleans the 2,000-object dataset, derives each asteroid's orbital ellipse and time-propagation elements, and flattens roughly 42,000 Earth close-approach events into a form our visualizations can consume. On top of that data we have a working Three.js "Orbital Theater" that propagates every asteroid through time with Kepler's equation, lets the user play, scrub, and fast-forward the clock, and automatically holds at each asteroid's closest approach to Earth, alongside a D3 scatter of the population's orbital geometry.

2. What will be the most challenging part of your project to design and why?

The most challenging aspect of our project will probably be seamlessly integrating the 3D Three.js environment with the 2D D3.js interactive scrollytelling elements. Rendering thousands of orbital trajectories and propagating them every frame poses a real performance risk, so we are carefully balancing detailed rocky meshes for the close approachers against a lightweight animated point cloud for the rest of the population. Additionally, choreographing the camera transitions that move fluidly from a macro view of the near-Earth asteroid population down to an isolated close approach requires complex state management and spatial mathematics. We anticipate that keeping the user oriented during these dynamic zooms, while simultaneously synchronizing the simulation clock, the timeline scrubber, and the informative close-approach annotations, will require heavy iteration on the user experience design.
